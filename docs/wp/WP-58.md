# WP-58: bind-planner cost on Jolt clang-cl (R-62)

Branch `wp/58-plan-cost`, one PR. ADR-0046, section WP-58. Review: medium. No new diagnostic id, no rule change: every
verdict of the planner is the one main reaches (see "Verdicts unchanged").

## Where the time went (main, corpus 46102940336, jolt clang-cl)

The two largest reloads are header fan-outs: `header-fanout-update-context` plans 7 units (418 ms), and
`header-fanout-template-reference` plans 19 units (865 ms). `plan_ms` is the sum of the units' plan stages. Against the run
before WP-51 (corpus 46031985263: 336 / 709 ms), every stage of those reloads was slower on that runner, including the
ones the planner does not run:

| Stage of the reload | before WP-51 (46031985263) | main (46102940336) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `compile_ms` (clang-cl, another process), 7 / 19 units | 5846 / 11796 | 7644 / 15277 | 1.31 / 1.30 |
| `link_ms` (lld-link, another process) | 346 / 867 | 429 / 1061 | 1.24 / 1.22 |
| `anchor_ms` (in the broker, unchanged code) | 41.2 / 85.7 | 44.9 / 92.5 | 1.09 / 1.08 |
| `plan_ms` | 336 / 709 | 418 / 865 | 1.24 / 1.22 |

The broker logs the same records in both runs (87,891 `bind:` trace lines, 34,328 `narrow:` trace lines, 34,364 `reload:`
debug lines), so WP-51..55 added no measurable work there; most of the increase is the runner. The cost itself is in the
log. The corpus runs the Jolt cells at `trace` (`project.json: "trace": true`), and the broker wrote and flushed every record
from the thread that logged it. The loop in `pipeline.cpp` that logs one debug line per planned hook, and does nothing else,
logged 2,250 lines in 12 ms on that runner: about 5.3 µs per record. The largest unit, `unity_12_cxx.obj` (105 ms), logs
about 5,800 records in the bind planner and 4,500 more in narrowing and after it, so about half of its plan stage was
spent writing the log:

| `unity_12_cxx.obj`, main | ms | records logged | of it, the log at 5.3 µs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bind_ms` | 66.0 | ~5,800 (`bind:` decisions, candidates, fix-ups) | ~31 |
| `narrow_ms` | 21.7 | 2,250 (`narrow:` per hook) | ~12 |
| `rest_ms` | 11.4 | 2,250 (`reload: hook ...` per hook) | ~12 |

## What was learned

* The WP-50 split showed `bind_ms` but not that half of it was the log: at trace every decision is a record, and a record
  written and flushed by the thread that logs it costs more than the decision.
* Between the two corpus runs the compiler and the linker, other processes, were 22-31% slower; a reload's `plan_ms` should
  be compared within one run, or with the stage timings.

## Deferred

* The trace records themselves (one per decision, plan section 7) are kept; at trace their formatting is still paid
  by the planner's thread.
* `pipeline.cpp` looks up decisions by name with `BindPlan::find_decision` (a walk) in the link and load stages too (the
  undefined externals of an object, the code records of a patch); those stages are not the plan stage and were left alone.

## After the medium review of 6c19f6a (ADR-0046 rulings)

The planner indexes were accepted as equivalent to the walks they replace. The findings concern the log hub.

| # | Finding | Fix | Test (`unit.broker_core`, `test_events_and_log.cpp`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data race on shutdown: `write()` and `flush()` read `writer_.joinable()` under the mutex while `~LogHub` joined the thread without it | The mode is decided once in the constructor (`const bool background_`). `write()` and `flush()` test it together with `writer_done_` (under the mutex) and never touch `writer_`. The writer is stopped by `stop_writer()`, which the destructor calls | `a record logged while the background writer stops ...`: another thread logs 4,000 records (some at Warn) while `stop_writer()` runs; run under TSan |
| 2 | During shutdown a late record was written directly, ahead of the last batch the writer still held | While the writer runs, a late record goes on the queue, and the writer drains the queue before it exits. Only once the writer has set `writer_done_` (under the mutex, after its last write) does a record get written by its own thread, under the mutex | the same test: every record once, in the logging thread's order, and a record logged after the stop comes after them |
| 3 | `log_lines` and `bind_log_lines` also counted the records of other threads | `log_records_written()` counts per thread (`thread_local`) | — |
| 4 | `flush()` set `urgent_` when nothing was pending | `urgent_` is set only when records are pending, and is cleared when the wait returns with an empty queue | — |
| 5 | (pre-existing) The early returns of `run_headless` left the process-wide sink pointing at the destroyed hub | `LogSinkScope`, declared after the hub, installs it and puts the default sink back on every return path | `a LogSinkScope puts the default sink back on an early return` |
| 6 | (pre-existing) `seq` and `t_ms` were taken before the lock, so `seq` did not rise in the file | Both are taken under the mutex. A text line's body is still put together before the lock; only its head (time stamp and level) is formatted under it | `the sequence numbers of the file rise, with several threads logging` (JSON, 4 threads, 2,000 records) |

The synthetic profile at trace with the background writer is unchanged (bind about 95 ms against about 100 before the
round), and the verdict comparison with main still gives equal files for every case.
