# WP-54: a reload across several modules of one process applies nothing when one module is refused (R-56; ADR-0045)

Branch `wp/54-cross-module-atomic`, one PR. Diagnostic range of ADR-0045: CML3656–3669 (WP-47 used CML3650–3655). Used:
**CML3656**, **CML3657**, **CML3660**, **CML3661** (warnings) and **CML3658**, **CML3659** (errors), in
`broker/cml_core/include/cml/core/atomic_reload_diag_ids.h`. Review: high (pipeline, hooks).

## The defect

WP-47 made a reload all or nothing over the compilands of ONE module. The reload service still ran the modules of a reload one after
the other, each to the end: a header edit that recompiled a translation unit of the exe and one of a plugin DLL applied the exe's patch
(and published its `patch_applied`) before the DLL's translation unit was even planned; when that one was refused (a new
`thread_local`, CML3400) the reply was the refusal, the exe ran the edit and the DLL did not ("(1 of 2 modules failed)"). WP-47's
"Deferred" names it.

## What was built

| Piece | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Two phases per module | `broker/cml_core/src/pipeline.cpp`, `pipeline.h` (`ModuleReload`) | `Run::run()` is split into `prepare_steps()` (sweep, compile, WP-47's prepare of every compiland) and `apply_steps()` + `finish()` (WP-47's apply loop, re-baselining, the batch, `patch_applied`). What the old function kept in locals between the two (the sweep, the units, the PDB, the generation before, the report) are members. `abandon(why)` gives back the staged compilands' address space and re-baselines nothing (as after a refusal of its own). With `events_held` the `patch_applied` events are kept for the caller. `patch_module()` is the two phases back to back, unchanged in behaviour (now `const`). |
| The modules of one process | `broker/cml_core/src/process_reload.cpp`, `process_reload.h` (`reload_process`) | One call per process. One module: `patch_module()` exactly as before. Several: (1) `prepare()` every module in order; a refusal stops there: the modules prepared before it are abandoned, the ones after it are not prepared (not even compiled), **CML3656** is published and the refusal's message ends with `(module 2 of 2, plugin.dll); no module of process N was applied`. (2) `apply()` in order; when module k fails (its own compilands already dealt with by WP-47) the modules applied before it are rolled back with `revert_module(..., Last, only_reload = this reload)`, newest first, quiet (no `patch_reverted`, nothing was announced), each with at least 10 s (the reload's deadline may be what failed): **CML3657**; a refused roll-back is **CML3658** (that module's batch stays for `revert`), an unconfirmed one (CML5852 / CML5855) **CML3659**. The agent is gone: nothing can be rolled back; the applied modules stay recorded, their baseline goes back to what it was (`restore_baseline` of their batch) and nothing is announced (**CML3661**). |

### Diagnostics (CML3656–3669)

| id | severity | meaning | what to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3656 | warning | a module of the process was refused while preparing: no module of the process was applied | fix what the refusal names, reload |
| 3657 | warning | applying a module failed after other modules of the process were applied: they were rolled back | fix what the failure names, reload |
| 3658 | error | applying a module failed and rolling back an earlier module failed too: the process runs a part of the edit | `revert` (scope last), or restart |
| 3659 | error | the roll-back of an earlier module was sent but not confirmed (CML5852, CML5855) | reach `SyncPoint()`; restart if hooks of rolled-back patches keep running |
| 3660 | warning | a module's hooks were not confirmed (CML5309) or not taken out (CML4656): the modules before it stay applied, the rest is not applied | reach `SyncPoint()`, reload, or `revert` |
| 3661 | warning | the agent disconnected while the modules were applied: the applied ones stay recorded, nothing re-baselined or announced | restart; or enable again and reload |

CML3662–3669 are unused. Logged at trace: `reload: module plugin.dll (module 2 of 2 of process 4711) refused (CML3200): no module of
the process is applied (...)`, `... prepared`, `reload: all 2 modules of process 4711 are prepared; applying`, `... rolling back the 1
module(s) this reload applied before it`, `reload: app.exe is not applied: ...`.

## How to run

* Unit (portable, also on Linux with `-DCML_ALLOW_NON_WINDOWS=ON`): `ctest -R unit.broker_core`, the `cross-module reload (WP-54)`
  cases in `tests/unit/broker/test_reload.cpp`, one per failure point:
  * prepare refused in the second module (plugin.dll's main.cpp cannot be planned, CML3200): app.exe was linked and never loaded,
    CML3656, nothing live in either module, app.exe not re-baselined; the fixed edit then patches both, app.exe at the slice it was
    linked into before, `objects_held_peak` 1;
  * prepare refused in the first module: plugin.dll is not even compiled;
  * apply failing in the second module (the agent's hook-install stage answers CML4652 for plugin.dll's InstallHooks): plugin.dll is
    unloaded, app.exe's hook is reverted to the original bytes and its image un-committed, CML3657, no `patch_reverted`;
  * rollback failing (the revert of app.exe's hook is refused, CML5851): CML3658, app.exe stays live and recorded, `revert last` undoes it;
  * rollback not confirmed (the revert's InstallHooks gets no answer): CML3659, the broker's state follows the roll-back;
  * plugin.dll's hooks unconfirmed (CML5309): CML3660, app.exe stays applied and announced, nothing is reverted;
  * the agent lost at plugin.dll's LoadPatch: CML3661, nothing rolled back or announced;
  * two processes: a link refusal in the second (app.exe only) leaves both modules of the first applied, no CML3656;
  * a module with nothing to compile next to a refused one: the refusal reads as for one module (no CML3656, no suffix).

  Seven of the nine fail on main (the last two document behaviour that stays as it was). The assertion that fails on main in the first
  case: `CHECK(rig.seen<ipc::LoadPatch>().empty())` (main loaded app.exe's patch before plugin.dll was planned), and with it
  `generation_of_module(*m) == 0` (1 on main), `rig.events->count("patch_applied") == 0` (1 on main) and `diag_count(rig, "CML3656",
  "warning") == 1`.
* Harness: `ctest -R unit.harness` (`test_wp54_scenarios.cpp`: the scenario's shape).
* Scenario: `cmltest --scenario tests/harness/scenarios/cross_module_atomic.toml ...`. Fixture `cross_module_atomic`: `scale.h` (no
  code: `kScale`, `CML_PLUGIN_TLS`) is included through `shared.h` by `exe_part.cpp` (the exe) and `plugin.cpp` (the plugin DLL, which
  also has an unedited `thread_local` in `plugin_tls.cpp`); `TICK exe=11 plugin=101 frames=N`. Edit `scale_2_tls` (kScale 1 -> 2, and
  a new `thread_local` in plugin.cpp): the reload is refused with CML3400, no `patch_applied` arrives (`expect_event ... absent =
  true`), `exe=11 plugin=101` go on. Before WP-54 the exe (prepared and applied first: its path sorts first) would have printed
  `exe=22`. Edit `stacked/no_tls` (the `thread_local` gone, kScale stays 2): both modules reload (`modules_patched = 2`,
  `processes_patched = 1`, `objects_held_peak = 1`), `exe=22 plugin=202`, and the DLL's `thread_local` frame counter never went
  back (`expect_monotonic frames`).
* `atomic_reload` (one module enabled) goes through `patch_module()`; `plugin_reload` and `plugin_late_load` (the exe and the plugin
  enabled, the edit in the plugin only) go through `reload_process()` with an exe that has nothing to compile, which reads exactly as
  before (their scenarios are unchanged and stay green on the matrix: see the PR).

## Memory

A module's prepare drops every compiland's COFF object once it is linked (WP-47's `release_prepared`), so while the next module of the
process is prepared the earlier one holds none: the reload holds one object at a time over all modules. What a prepared module keeps
until it is applied is what WP-47's staged compilands keep (fix-ups, hook table, code records, registry entries) and its PDB mapping
(as during one module's reload). The reply's new top-level `objects_held_peak` is the most any module held at once; the scenario
asserts 1 (unit test too). The working set of a fan-out over many DLLs was not measured on Windows in this round.

## Decisions taken here

* **Stop at the first refused module.** As WP-47 stops at the first refused compiland: the modules after it are not compiled, so the
  reply names one refusal; the next reload compiles them (their files were not re-baselined).
* **The modules of a process with nothing to compile** keep their (empty) report; they are not "failed". When no other module of the
  process had anything to apply (the usual reload of a plugin next to an unchanged exe), a refusal or failure reads exactly as for one
  module: no CML365x of WP-54, no suffix.
* **Per-module results of the other modules** are errors with the CML365x id (they count in "(n of m modules failed)"); only one
  warning per process is published, and the reply's error is the refusal or failure itself (its own id), so scenarios and clients that
  expect CML3400 / CML3214 keep working.
* **A module's own CML3652 / CML3654** (its own roll-back failed or was not confirmed) still rolls the earlier modules back: they are
  separate images, and putting them back brings the process closer to what it ran before.
* **WP-21 restart / WP-45 re-apply** go through the same `run_reload`, so a hot-restart that re-applies the session's edits to several
  modules of the new process is now all or nothing per process as well.

## What was learned

* WP-47's pipeline already had both phases; the only obstacle to running them for several modules was that their state lived in the
  locals of one function. Moving them to members made the split mechanical, and `patch_module()` stays the same code path.
* Modules of one process do not plan against each other's staged patches (each has its own registry; cross-module references bind to
  the other module's file through its exports), so no overlay across modules is needed: preparing module B while module A is staged
  gives the same plan as before.
* The WP-22 revert is per module and already restores the baseline, the registry and the generation, so a roll-back of a module is
  exactly `revert last` limited to this reload's id.

## Deferred

* The modules after a refused one are not prepared, so a second refusal in another module is only reported by the next reload.
* As in WP-47: initialisers that ran in a rolled-back module are not undone, and the rolled-back patch DLLs stay loaded and inert.
* The WP-22 revert restores only the baseline stamps the module had before; a file the module's baseline did not know before the reload
  keeps its new stamp after a roll-back (pre-existing revert behaviour; such a file is the reload's own change, so the next sweep may not
  see it as changed). Not changed here.
