### Variants (`spike.cpp`), where clang records the static and what the inline sites say

| Case | Record of the static | Inline sites of the caller |
|---|---|---|
| internal `static` helper `b_helper` with `static bx`, inlined into `b_caller` | `S_LDATA32 bx` directly in `b_caller` | `b_helper` (LF_FUNC_ID) |
| `inline` (external) `c_helper` with `static cx`, inlined | `S_GDATA32 cx` in the globals stream, nothing in the caller | `c_helper` (LF_FUNC_ID) |
| lambda of `d_g`, both inlined into `d_caller` | `S_LDATA32 dx` directly in `d_caller` | `d_g` > `<lambda_8>@?0??d_g@@...::operator()` (nested; the closure is d_g's, not d_caller's) |
| `extern "C" e_main`: own `counter` + inlined lambda's `ex` | both directly in `e_main`, plain names, own first | `<lambda_0>@?0??e_main@@9@::operator()` (C linkage: `?e_main@@9`) |
| nested lambdas in `g_caller` | `gx` directly in `g_caller` | operator() > operator() of `<lambda_1>@?0???R0?0??g_caller@@...` |
| lambda inlined in `h_caller` and also used as a function pointer | `hx` in BOTH `h_caller` and `<lambda_2>::__invoke` (same address) | operator() in both |
| own static `own` + inlined lambda without statics (`i_caller`) | `own` directly in `i_caller` | operator() |
| `rt(A1*)` / `rt(A2*)` overloads, lambdas converted to function pointers | `rtti` in each | none |

S_INLINESITE layout: parent, end, inlinee (IPI index), annotations; S_INLINESITE2 adds an invocation count before the
annotations. clang also writes `S_INLINEES` (0x1168) per procedure; cl is not known to, so the walk of the inline sites
is the source.

### What the data says

1. Telling "f really inlined a lambda" from "f has a closure" is possible per procedure, from the old procedure's own
   inline-site tree, without any display-name match: the procedure is the candidate record's parent (its address), so
   overloads and C linkage need no lookup at all.
2. "Of its own closures" (ADR-0044's wording) is too narrow: a lambda of another function inlined through it (`d_g`'s
   lambda in `d_caller`) records its static in the outermost procedure too. The rule should take any closure.
3. A call operator reaches a caller also through `__invoke`, and templates over a closure are internal-linkage functions
   whose statics land in the caller the same way.
4. **Beyond R-54 (not lambdas):** an internal-linkage helper inlined into its caller records its statics in the caller
   (`bx`). (a) Deleting the helper and adding `static bx` to the caller pairs it wrongly, as in R-54; (b) keeping the
   helper, its static finds no record in a procedure of its own and is decided new. Neither is covered by a lambda rule;
   an inlinee's linkage cannot be read from LF_FUNC_ID (`b_helper` and `c_helper` look the same).
